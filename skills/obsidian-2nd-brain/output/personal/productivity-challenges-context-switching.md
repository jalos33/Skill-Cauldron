## Summary

Research and reflection on how context switching destroys productivity - with practical countermeasures.

## Details

**The Research:**

According to productivity studies:
- Switching tasks can cost 20-40% of productive time
- Deep focus requires 15-25 minutes to achieve after a disruption
- Multiple concurrent contexts dramatically increase cognitive load

**Personal Observations:**

Tracking my own patterns revealed:
- Morning context switches (before deep work begins) are most costly
- Notifications during focus blocks cause lasting distraction (up to 23 min recovery)
- Task batching reduces switching frequency significantly

### Key Points

1. **Batch similar tasks together** - Email at set times, calls in blocks
2. **Notification discipline is non-negotiable** - Turn off all but critical alerts during deep work
3. **Respect the transition cost** - Schedule buffer time between high-focus and administrative tasks

## Takeaways

Context switching costs were a major productivity killer I underestimated. The key insight: protecting focus time isn't just about doing more - it's about avoiding the hidden tax that fragmentation imposes on every switch.
